# Current Research Papers

This repository brings together two research works by Dr. M. Abdul Qadeer and Shahbaaz Ahmad, both focused on governance, risk, and human factors in digital systems.

## About the collection

The projects in this workspace reflect two complementary research directions:

1. AI-enabled governance, risk, and compliance
2. Behavioral cyber risk governance and the psychology of hacking

Together, they show a broader pattern: modern risk management must account not only for technical controls, but also for governance design, regulatory alignment, and human behavior.

## Papers in this repository

### 1) AI-GRC Integrated Management Framework

Folder: [AIGRC-IMF](AIGRC-IMF/)

Title: Artificial Intelligence in Governance, Risk, and Compliance: An Integrated Framework Grounded in ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and ISO/IEC 42001:2023

This work argues that organizations using AI in governance, risk, and compliance functions need a framework that integrates both information security governance and AI governance. It proposes the AI-GRC IMF as a five-phase model:

- Identify
- Assess
- Control
- Deploy
- Monitor

The framework connects ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and ISO/IEC 42001:2023, and it aligns with GDPR and the EU AI Act. Its central contribution is practical: it helps organizations extend their existing ISMS rather than build a parallel governance structure from scratch.

Key themes:
- AI risk governance in regulated environments
- ISO/IEC 27001 + ISO/IEC 42001 integration
- GDPR and EU AI Act alignment
- Bias, accountability, privacy, and model governance
- Risk-to-control mapping for AI-enabled GRC systems

### 2) Beyond the Hoodie: Hacker Psychology and the Ethical-Criminal Divide

Folder: [Beyond_the_Hoodie](Beyond_the_Hoodie/)

Title: Beyond the Hoodie: Hacker Psychology and the Ethical-Criminal Divide — A Behavioral Cyber Risk Governance Perspective

This paper shifts attention from tools and systems to the human dimension of cyber risk. It argues that the difference between an ethical hacker and a cybercriminal is not simply technical capability, but a behavioral continuum shaped by:

- digital environment
- moral disengagement
- motivational needs
- organizational culture

The paper introduces the Behavioral Cyber Risk Governance Framework (BCRGF), which organizes governance principles across three dimensions:

- Environmental
- Moral
- Motivational

The core argument is that cyber risk governance is incomplete if it ignores psychological and behavioral drivers of harmful conduct.

Key themes:
- Hacker psychology
- Online disinhibition
- Moral disengagement
- Self-determination theory
- Human-centered cyber governance
- Ethical vs criminal behavior as a governable continuum

## Why these papers matter

Together, these works address a shared concern: risk is not only a technical or compliance problem. It is also a governance, behavioral, and accountability problem.

- The AI-GRC paper focuses on formal governance structures for AI in regulated organizations.
- The behavioral cyber paper focuses on the human conditions that produce risk in the first place.

Both emphasize that effective governance must be designed around the actual drivers of harm, not only the controls that appear on paper.
