## The Problem This Paper Addresses

Regulated industries have been folding AI into governance, risk, and compliance functions faster than their governance architectures can handle. Most organisations deploying AI in GRC contexts have no formal mechanism to evaluate bias, assign accountability for automated decisions, or align their deployments with the regulatory requirements now coming into force under the EU AI Act.

Two international standards exist that, when read together, provide a governance architecture capable of addressing exactly this. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 governs the security of the information assets AI systems depend on. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the first standard written specifically for AI management systems. Both share the ISO High-Level Structure and were built to integrate. Despite that, their combined application to GRC has attracted almost no academic attention. That gap is harder to explain the longer it persists. This paper addresses it directly.

## The AI-GRC IMF at a Glance

The **AI-GRC Integrated Management Framework (AI-GRC IMF)** is a five-phase lifecycle model:

```
Phase 1: IDENTIFY  →  AI system inventory + EU AI Act risk classification
Phase 2: ASSESS    →  Dual risk assessment (ISO 27001 §8.2 + ISO 42001 §6.1) + GDPR DPIA
Phase 3: CONTROL   →  Integrated Annex A control selection + extended SoA
Phase 4: DEPLOY    →  Conformity assessment + staged rollout + change management
Phase 5: MONITOR   →  Unified Clause 9 programme + bias tracking + regulatory scanning
```

The framework builds on an existing ISMS. Organisations already certified to ISO/IEC 27001 extend what they have. They do not build a second management system from scratch. Building on what already exists is not a compromise. It is the condition under which governance gets done.

## Three Supporting Artefacts

The paper includes three tables built for direct practitioner use.

**Table I: AI Use-Case Taxonomy.** Maps eight AI capabilities to GRC function domains and applicable standards. They are: NLP regulatory scanning, predictive risk modelling, fraud and anomaly detection, compliance document monitoring, SIEM/UEBA security analytics, executive risk dashboards, internal audit automation, and LLM policy assistants.

**Table II: Governance Instrument Comparison.** Side-by-side analysis of ISO/IEC 27001:2022, ISO/IEC 42001:2023, NIST AI RMF 1.0, GDPR, EU AI Act 2024/1689, and ISO/IEC 27701:2019 across scope, GRC relevance, AI risk focus, and integration pathway.

**Table III: Risk-to-Control Mapping.** Seven AI risk categories, each mapped to specific Annex A controls from both standards, with regulatory requirements and mitigation techniques. These are: algorithmic bias, privacy infringement, adversarial attacks, model drift, explainability deficit, accountability deficit, and third-party supply-chain risk.

## Methodology

- **Type:** PRISMA-guided systematic literature review + normative document analysis
- **Sources reviewed:** 47, spanning January 2018 to March 2026
- **Databases:** IEEE Xplore, Scopus, ScienceDirect, ACM Digital Library, official ISO / NIST / EU Commission repositories
- **Standards analysed at clause level:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022, ISO/IEC 42001:2023, ISO/IEC 27701:2019, NIST AI RMF 1.0, GDPR 2016/679, EU AI Act 2024/1689

## Regulatory Alignment

| Regulation / Standard | Applicability | Framework Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| GDPR 2016/679 | In force | DPIA integration, data minimisation, Art. 22 rights |
| EU AI Act 2024/1689 | Fully applicable August 2027 | High-risk classification, conformity, post-market monitoring |
| ISO/IEC 27001:2022 | Current | Security foundation, ISMS extension |
| ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Current | AI management system, bias controls, human oversight |
| NIST AI RMF 1.0 | Current | Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage function mapping |
